Case Study1.1

Paytrust AI, a company processing digital payments in Pakistan. The AI technology team reports
great success: their fraud detection model achieved a 96% accuracy rate during development.
They wish to release it immediately. The product is submitted to the Quality assurance team to
assure quality of the product before it goes up. However, the Quality assurance team gives a
different picture. There are cases when legitimate payments were blocked. Think about paying for
medicines or transferring money to your family, just to see how the transaction gets declined.
Although the algorithm works effectively and catches the fraudulent activity, it can be causing
troubles for legitimate customers as well. Moreover, there is a matter of PayTrust AI using
another algorithm to approve loan requests. It looks like there are two groups of clients getting
approved at significantly different rates. However, nobody has proved yet where the discrepancy
originates: from the clients themselves, data, algorithms or decision-making process. You are
part of the AI governance review committee. The Chief Technology Officer asks you what steps
should be taken next: release the product, conduct a small controlled pilot or halt the release
process altogether. Your task is to analyze the facts and come up with a decision that would
satisfy both client and auditor.

How the lab works
The accompanying Colab notebook provides you with transactions that are simulated, a fraud
detection model, the cost of errors, loan approval statistics, governance, assumptions about
human reviews and monitoring. Go through it step by step. The numbers are instructional
illustrations and not proof of anything in the financial services industry.
Important: the fraud detection system and loan approval system need separate decisions. A good fraud
result does not automatically make the loan system safe.
